# Fate’s Hand Tarot — Major Arcana Art Bible

**Status:** production art bible and integration contract, grounded in the current Nymedes Vault canon  
**Scope:** the 22 Major Arcana only, numbered 0–XXI  
**Primary language on cards:** English  
**Tarot framework:** Rider–Waite–Smith order and symbolic logic, reinterpreted through the Saints of AvA

---

## 1. Core concept

The Fate’s Hand Major Arcana are **not Nymedean characters pasted onto a Tarot deck**.

In Nymedes, the Saints of AvA are the historical and metaphysical origin of the 22 Major Arcana. Each card is therefore simultaneously:

1. a recognisable and functional Tarot archetype;
2. the sacred image of a Saint of AvA;
3. a fragment of that Saint’s mortal crisis and transcendence;
4. a doorway into the territory, relic, memory or state that the Saint left within the “O” and the White Void;
5. a Fate’s Hand game object capable of awakening a character to an Arcane Destiny.

The traditional Tarot meaning always comes first. Nymedes supplies the person, setting and metaphysical explanation.

> **Design law:** preserve the symbolic function of the traditional card, but never copy the Rider–Waite–Smith composition literally.

## 2. Canon extracted from the Vault

### AvA and the Saints

- **AvA** is the principle of absolution: the voluntary dissolution of the self into the Whole.
- The Saints were once embodied beings, principally humans of the First Age.
- In the mythological language of Nymedes, spiritual awakening is shown through growing white wings and a sphere of white energy above the head.
- Transcendence dissolves the body into light and feeds the “O”, the field through which consciousness can affect reality.
- The 22 Saints correspond exactly to the 22 Major Arcana, 0–XXI.
- **Karagall is the fallen Saint**, Arcane XV, who rejected normal dissolution and imposed the Moisson instead.
- A Major Arcana awakening in Fate’s Hand is not metaphorical: it creates a bond between a character and one of these cosmic patterns.

### Locked special correspondences

- **XV — The Devil:** Karagall, the Devourer of Souls.
- **XVIII — The Moon:** Ur-Nozgul, the Mirror of Dreams.
- **XIX — The Sun:** Aulmyria, the Inner Sun.
- **XI — Justice:** Šegazursu, the Sword with the Bloodied Pommel.
- **XII — The Hanged Man:** Nis-Khazû is the reference Saint; Bozetius is a hidden alternative incarnation.

### Telva and the two faces

- **Telva is the celestial body**, the golden moon. Telva is not a Saint.
- **Aulmyria** is the golden dragoness and the illuminated face of Telva: Arcane XIX, The Sun.
- **Aulmyra** is the human lover, muse and confidante of Aulmyria.
- Aulmyra transcended as **Ur-Nozgul**, the hidden face of Telva: Arcane XVIII, The Moon.
- Aulmyria and Aulmyra are distinct beings despite their nearly identical names.

### Important correction to the first Moon prototype

The attractive black, antique-gold and silver-mist treatment should be retained, but the five moons must not remain the central idea of XVIII.

**The Moon is not “the five moons of Nymedes.” It is Ur-Nozgul and the dark face of Telva.**

The correct card must focus on one celestial body with two inseparable faces, reflected light, dream, disguise, intuition and truths revealed indirectly.

### Canon spelling

This bible uses **Ur-Nozgul**, the spelling in the Vault’s canonical index and dedicated file. The spelling `Ur’Nozgull` appeared in the creative request but is not presently the Vault spelling.

## 4. Visual identity of the complete deck

### Format

- Exact vertical Tarot proportion: **7:12**.
- The generated illustration is **full bleed and textless**.
- A narrow, consistent vector frame is composited after generation.
- The Roman numeral is composited at the top.
- The standard English card title is composited at the bottom.
- No other mandatory text on the card face.
- The Saint’s name belongs in the guidebook, not as a large second title.
- Keep all indispensable faces, hands and symbols clear of the outer 6% on every side, because that zone is partly covered by the fixed frame.

### Medium and finish

- Sophisticated hand-painted dark fantasy.
- Painterly realism combined with the precision of an engraved sacred manuscript.
- Fine linework, aged pigments, restrained gilding and tactile blackened surfaces.
- Premium physical-deck appearance, not a glossy mobile-game card.
- High detail but a simple, readable silhouette at thumbnail size.

### Base palette

- blackened indigo;
- charcoal;
- antique gold;
- tarnished silver;
- bone ivory;
- muted mineral colours;
- restrained crimson only where narratively justified.

The first prototype’s black, antique-gold and moonlit-silver atmosphere is approved as the deck’s base language.

### Saintly visual grammar

All Saints should appear to belong to the same lost First Age civilisation:

- austere ceremonial clothing combining ancient sacred silhouettes with subtle advanced construction;
- ivory, charcoal and oxidised gold rather than generic medieval-European costume;
- geometric jewellery and octagonal details;
- no Christian crosses, church mitres or Renaissance-European crowns unless transformed into original Nymedean forms;
- faces and bodies must be individual, human and emotionally credible;
- luminous wings are made of fine “O” filaments or refracted light, never ordinary feathered angel wings;
- a small sphere of white energy above or behind the head can indicate awakening;
- bodily edges may dissolve into luminous threads when transcendence is present;
- the transcendent state should feel beautiful but also like a genuine loss of individual embodiment.

### Border system

The border is a **single deterministic vector asset**, never regenerated with the illustration. Use the same border architecture on all 22 cards:

- blackened engraved frame with worn antique-gold linework;
- an octagonal upper cartouche for the Roman numeral;
- **22 tiny orbital spheres** integrated around the frame;
- 21 spheres are pale ivory or dormant silver;
- one sphere may appear bruised crimson or black-red, representing Karagall’s corrupted satellite;
- subtle “O” filaments connect some spheres without creating visual clutter;
- one small Saint-specific sigil at the base;
- no universal red gemstones or open-hand emblems: those belonged to the prototype exploration and are not the deck’s primary canon.

The compositor, not the image model, is responsible for:

- the exact frame geometry;
- the count and placement of all 22 spheres;
- the Roman numeral;
- the English title;
- the font, tracking, size and safe margins;
- identical placement across the full deck.

### Light as metaphysics

Use three clearly different forms of light:

1. **White light:** ÂNON, AvA, absolution, the White Void and the “O”.
2. **Gold light:** Aulmyria, inner clarity, revelation, equitable warmth.
3. **Crimson light:** Karagall, forced transcendence, hunger, the Moisson and the Crimson Shroud.

These lights must never be treated as interchangeable decorative effects.

## 6. The 22 card directives

### 0 — THE FOOL

**Saint:** En-Zalûm, *the Walker of the Void*  
**Tarot core:** beginning, innocence, trust, freedom, risk; shadow: recklessness, dissociation, refusal to see danger.

```text
Show En-Zalûm stepping from the last intact stone of the known world into a luminous tear of White Void. He carries only a small wrapped bundle containing four barely visible emblems corresponding to the future Minor Arcana suits. A pale, alert companion-creature catches the hem of his garment: instinct warns him, but does not stop him. A white flower or equivalent fragile living symbol is held loosely in one hand. His face is lifted with lucid wonder, not stupidity. The abyss must read simultaneously as a fatal fall and the first step onto a road that cannot yet be seen. Use bright ivory against deep black-indigo, with a very distant unknown world taking shape inside the mist.
```

### I — THE MAGICIAN

**Saint:** Ur-Meshtu, *the Weaver of Realities*  
**Tarot core:** will, skill, manifestation, directing potential; shadow: manipulation, trickery, talent severed from purpose.

```text
Show Ur-Meshtu before a First Age ritual table bearing four original Nymedean objects that clearly fulfil the roles of wand, cup, sword and pentacle. One hand reaches upward into the “O”; the other directs luminous threads into matter below, preserving the traditional “above and below” gesture without copying it literally. An unbroken loop of light forms briefly above the Saint. Flowers or living growth emerge where the filaments touch the world, while one darker thread quietly coils around a hidden finger to suggest manipulation. The scene must communicate focused agency: potential becomes reality because consciousness gives it form.
```

### II — THE HIGH PRIESTESS

**Saint:** Še-Nirgal, *the Guardian of the Enigma*  
**Tarot core:** hidden knowledge, intuition, silence, threshold, the unrevealed; shadow: secrecy, withdrawal, inaccessible or superficial knowledge.

```text
Place Še-Nirgal seated between two non-identical monoliths, one pale and one black, guarding a translucent veil embroidered with the unseen architecture of the “O”. Behind the veil lies deep living water and an impossible night garden, sensed more than fully seen. She holds a partly concealed tablet or scroll of First Age memory, never displaying the whole truth. A crescent of reflected silver light rests at her feet. Her gaze should meet the viewer calmly, as if she knows the question but will not answer it directly. The image must be still, symmetrical and inward, with one small flaw in the symmetry suggesting that the mystery is alive.
```

### III — THE EMPRESS

**Saint:** Ama-Telurra, *the Mother-Matrix*  
**Tarot core:** fertility, embodiment, creation, nurture, abundance; shadow: smothering, dependence, stagnation through comfort.

```text
Show Ama-Telurra enthroned within a fertile Nymedean valley where multiple forms of life emerge from one luminous underground matrix. Her seat is grown rather than built. A crown or halo of twelve small stars marks cyclical abundance, while grain, fruit, flowing water and pregnancy are evoked without reducing her to literal motherhood. One hand nurtures new life; the other rests possessively on the roots that bind everything to her. Include a shield-like emblem of generative union, redesigned as a Nymedean sigil. The mood is sensuous, protective and materially alive, with the faint suggestion that nothing beneath her canopy can grow entirely free of her.
```

### IV — THE EMPEROR

**Saint:** Dur-Lugal, *the Pillar of Stone*  
**Tarot core:** structure, authority, stability, protection, reason; shadow: rigidity, domination, immaturity hidden behind control.

```text
Show Dur-Lugal seated on a severe basalt throne fused into a high mountain, his posture forming the visual axis of the card. Replace conventional ram motifs with an original horned architectural pattern that performs the same role of force, command and uncompromising initiative. He holds a geometric sceptre and a small globe or model of ordered territory. Beneath the armour, show one glimpse of a human red garment or vulnerable hand: authority is constructed around mortality. The mountains protect a city below but also cast it into shadow. Do not establish any unconfirmed connection to the Araag Emperor; keep the figure a First Age Saint.
```

### V — THE HIEROPHANT

**Saint:** En-Kalama, *the Guide of Ways*  
**Tarot core:** tradition, teaching, initiation, shared belief, institutional knowledge; shadow: conformity, captivity, weak or over-kind authority.

```text
Show En-Kalama at the entrance to a sacred route made of branching luminous lines, teaching two initiates whose clothing and postures are visibly different. His blessing gesture must indicate transmission rather than command. Two crossed keys or equivalent paired instruments open an outer path and an inner path. Behind him stands an ancient institution of the First Age, beautiful but weighty, with repeated figures suggesting generations of tradition. One path is well lit and crowded; another is narrow and almost erased. The card must honour lineage while quietly asking whether the guide opens a way or decides which ways may exist.
```

### VI — THE LOVERS

**Saints:** Nin-Ahura and Ur-Damuz, *Bound by One Breath*  
**Tarot core:** love, attraction, union, values and consequential choice; shadow: division, frustrated union, choices made without alignment.

```text
Show Nin-Ahura and Ur-Damuz facing one another with distinct identities, sharing one visible breath of luminous “O” that joins but does not erase them. Behind each grows a different sacred tree or pillar, one bearing knowledge and one bearing flame or life. Above them, an impersonal AvA radiance witnesses rather than blesses their choice. Between the pair is a narrow third path leading toward a difficult mountain: love is not merely attraction but a decision about the life they will make. Their hands have not yet touched. The image must hold desire, freedom and the cost of choosing in the same suspended instant.
```

### VII — THE CHARIOT

**Saint:** Šul-Adûr, *the Herald of the Breakthrough*  
**Tarot core:** direction, will, victory, disciplined momentum; shadow: aggression, presumption, quarrel, loss of control.

```text
Show Šul-Adûr standing in a ceremonial vehicle of the First Age, moving without visible reins because opposing forces are controlled through will. Two guardian creatures or sculptural living forms—one pale, one black—pull in divergent directions while remaining aligned. A canopy of stars and a square protective armour motif retain the traditional celestial and material disciplines. The city behind him is the order he leaves; a ruptured barrier ahead is the breakthrough he seeks. His victory must feel real but precarious: the smallest break in concentration would turn triumph into violent division.
```

### VIII — STRENGTH

**Saint:** Nin-Telzûra, *the Unyielding Soul*  
**Tarot core:** courage, compassion, patience, mastery of instinct; shadow: force, abuse of power, weakness disguised as domination.

```text
Show Nin-Telzûra calmly touching the jaws or brow of a massive Nymedean predator. She does not defeat, chain or humiliate it; the animal lowers its violence because her presence can contain it. An unbroken loop of “O” light appears above her, while pale flowers grow around claws capable of tearing stone. Her physical stance is gentle and completely stable. In the predator’s shadow, briefly suggest the outline of a tyrant wielding the same power through fear. The card must make it unmistakable that true strength is intimate self-command, not victory over a weaker creature.
```

### IX — THE HERMIT

**Saint:** Uru-Zannir, *the Watcher of Stars*  
**Tarot core:** solitude, discernment, inner guidance, patient search; shadow: isolation, concealment, fearful withdrawal.

```text
Show Uru-Zannir alone on a dark high ridge above the cloud layer of Nymedes. His lantern contains a six-rayed white geometry or tiny star of “O”, illuminating only the next few steps rather than the whole world. A weathered staff grounds him. Far behind, traces of a civilisation remain visible, making clear that solitude is a chosen interval, not ignorance of others. His cloak partly merges with the night; the same darkness that gives him insight could also swallow him. Keep the composition spare, vertical and silent.
```

### X — WHEEL OF FORTUNE

**Saint:** Zigg-Damû, *the Turning One*  
**Tarot core:** destiny, cycles, reversals, luck, elevation and fall; shadow: excess, instability, resistance to change.

```text
Show Zigg-Damû suspended at the calm centre of an enormous rotating mechanism made from “O” filaments, ancient script and interlocking celestial rings. Figures or states of being rise on one side and descend on the other, while four still witnesses occupy the corners and study the cycle. Include a guardian of knowledge above the wheel and a serpentine or dissolving force descending, but redesign them as original Nymedean forms. Zigg-Damû must not turn the wheel; the Saint understands and inhabits the turning. The image must convey fortune as a real cosmic motion that no ruler owns.
```

### XI — JUSTICE

**Saint:** Šegazursu, *the Sword with the Bloodied Pommel*  
**Tarot core:** equity, truth, consequence, impartial judgment; shadow: vengeance, bias, legalism, excessive severity.

```text
Show Šegazursu seated or standing between two severe pillars, holding balanced scales in one hand and his canonical black sword with a blood-red pommel upright in the other. His face bears the exhaustion of someone who mistook vengeance for justice and finally understood the difference. Behind him, merge his White Void territory into the composition: statues turned east and a river of frozen blood. In the scales, one pan holds the memory of his murdered city; the other holds the innocent descendants of his enemies. The sword’s point should align with his own heart, recalling the self-sacrifice that liberated the souls claimed by Karagall. Use controlled red only in the pommel, river and one reflected line—never as ornamental gore.
```

### XII — THE HANGED MAN

**Saint:** Nis-Khazû, *the Suspended One*  
**Tarot core:** voluntary suspension, surrender, altered perspective, sacrifice, revelation; shadow: selfish delay, meaningless martyrdom, refusal to release.

```text
Show Nis-Khazû suspended by one foot from a living luminous structure shaped like a Tau or branching First Age tree. His other leg crosses behind it, preserving the traditional geometry. His expression is peaceful and intensely awake; this is chosen suspension, not torture. A white halo-sphere forms around the inverted head as the world below appears to rotate into a new truth. One hand remains clenched around a small possession not yet surrendered, carrying the card’s shadow. Keep Bozetius out of the principal image: Nis-Khazû is the reference Saint, while Bozetius belongs to a possible alternate or guidebook treatment.
```

### XIII — DEATH

**Saint:** Eresh-Seyna, *She Who Crosses*  
**Tarot core:** ending, irreversible transition, mortality, transformation; shadow: inertia, stagnation, fear that petrifies change.

```text
Show Eresh-Seyna crossing a devastated threshold on a pale mount or equivalent inexorable carrier. Her face is living and calm, while parts of her body reveal an inner skeletal or crystalline structure: death is a passage, not a monster. A black banner bears a white five-petalled life emblem. A fallen ruler, a supplicant, a child and a spiritual figure respond differently to her arrival, establishing that no status prevents transition. Between two distant structures, a white dawn rises. Avoid gore. The image must say that an ending is absolute, but what crosses the ending is not merely annihilated.
```

### XIV — TEMPERANCE

**Saint:** Telu-Nirid, *the Sacred Mixer*  
**Tarot core:** balance, moderation, synthesis, healing, accommodation; shadow: disunion, incompatible mixtures, competing interests.

```text
Show Telu-Nirid with one foot on mineral ground and one within luminous water, continuously pouring a bright essence between two unlike vessels. The liquid must travel at an impossible angle, showing conscious synthesis rather than ordinary mixing. On the Saint’s chest, place an original square-and-triangle harmony sigil. A narrow path leads toward a distant crown-shaped radiance between mountains. In the mixture, keep two colours just barely distinct: union has not erased difference. A small spill darkening the ground suggests that balance is active work and that some combinations become poison when forced.
```

### XV — THE DEVIL

**Saint:** Karagall, *the Devourer of Souls*  
**Tarot core:** bondage, appetite, fatality, material or psychological captivity, power gained through surrender; shadow: blindness, weakness, petty cruelty, inability to recognise one’s chains.

```text
Show Karagall in a recognisably human form at the moment his ascension becomes somatic and unstable. He stands upon a dark altar while his lower body and wings dissolve into the living Crimson Shroud. His red wings are the corrupted sign of awakening, made from smoke and consumed souls rather than feathers. Two followers stand below, connected to him by loose chains they could physically remove but do not; their chains feed luminous soul-orbs upward into the mist. Karagall offers immense power with one hand and consumes identity with the other. Behind the prophet, the shadow must be an insatiable absence rather than a horned monster. Use crimson, black and bruised gold; avoid copying Baphomet or generic satanic imagery.
```

### XVI — THE TOWER

**Saint:** Gal-Velshur, *the Breaker of False Truth*  
**Tarot core:** revelation through collapse, unforeseen catastrophe, destruction of false structure; shadow: oppression, imprisonment, delayed or internalised disaster.

```text
Show Gal-Velshur at the base of an impossibly tall First Age tower whose perfect upper geometry is struck by a white bolt of truth from outside the frame. A false crown or sealed doctrine is blasted away. Two figures fall, not as punishment but because the hierarchy that held them has ceased to exist. Flames shaped like separate letters or sparks of consciousness emerge from the broken windows. Gal-Velshur does not cause the lightning; the Saint breaks open a lower door, choosing truth while catastrophe reveals the scale of the lie. The ruin must be terrifying and liberating at once.
```

### XVII — THE STAR

**Saint:** Is-Tammeth, *the Celestial Offering*  
**Tarot core:** hope, renewal, openness, spiritual generosity, future orientation; shadow: abandonment, arrogance, depleted faith.

```text
Show Is-Tammeth kneeling unarmoured beside a quiet pool beneath one great eight-rayed star and seven smaller stars. The Saint pours luminous water from two vessels: one stream restores the pool and collective “O”; the other nourishes dry land and divides into five living channels. The body is vulnerable without being eroticised, expressing radical openness after the Tower’s collapse. A small bird or messenger rests in a transformed tree. One vessel is nearly empty, carrying the danger of self-erasure through endless giving. Use clear nocturnal blue, silver and pale gold; the mood is spacious and honest.
```

### XVIII — THE MOON

**Saint:** Ur-Nozgul, *the Mirror of Dreams*  
**Mortal identity:** Aulmyra, human lover and muse of Aulmyria  
**Tarot core:** reflected light, dream, intuition, uncertainty, hidden influence, deception and the path into the unknown; shadow: instability, fear, distortion and silence.

```text
Show one and only one dominant celestial body: Telva, the golden moon, with an illuminated edge and a vast hidden face. Within the dark face, Ur-Nozgul appears as the almost invisible profile of a human woman made from warm silver mist. She must not fight or command; she reveals what was already present. A winding path crosses a fluid White Void between two asymmetrical megalithic gates toward Telva. On either side of the path, one domesticated and one wild Nymedean guardian respond to the same reflected light, preserving the traditional polarity of civilised and instinctive fear. From a black mirror-pool in the foreground, an ancient pale creature or submerged memory begins to emerge, representing what rises from beneath consciousness. Conceal one crimson Karagall filament in the scene, unable to perceive Ur-Nozgul. The atmosphere is warm silver, black-indigo and faint muted gold: prophetic, intimate, unstable and revelatory. No five-moon arc, no generic moon goddess, no combat pose.
```

### XIX — THE SUN

**Saint:** Aulmyria, *the Inner Sun*  
**Nature:** golden dragoness, former Red God, voluntarily transcended Saint of AvA  
**Tarot core:** clarity, joy, vitality, success, openness and shared warmth; shadow: diminished joy, exposure without wisdom, punitive certainty.

```text
Show Aulmyria as a majestic golden dragoness whose body is partly dissolving into equitable gold-white “O” light. The apparent sun of the card must radiate from within her chest or heart as the Inner Sun—not from the physical white sun of Nymedes, which is ÂNON. Before her, a joyful unarmoured child or newly awakened mortal rides or stands with a pale living creature, holding a red-gold banner of liberated vitality. A low wall behind them has been safely crossed, and tall solar flowers turn toward Aulmyria’s inner radiance. Her light touches every figure and object equally. In one edge of the image, the same light becomes a thin judicial blade, remembering the vengeful certainty she transcended. Include the bright face of Telva only as a subtle golden crescent or reflection, never as a second physical sun.
```

### XX — JUDGEMENT

**Saint:** Nam-Ruhar, *the Call of Origin*  
**Tarot core:** awakening, reckoning, renewal, answer to a higher call, final evaluation; shadow: hesitation, weak response, fear of irreversible decision.

```text
Show Nam-Ruhar suspended above a cold expanse, sounding an original First Age instrument whose note becomes visible concentric waves of white “O”. Below, people of different ages rise from stone chambers, drowned cities or discarded former identities with arms open to the call. Their small awakening spheres ignite one by one. Distant mountains or an impassable horizon show that this is not a return to ordinary life but an irreversible change of state. One figure remains half-seated, unable to decide whether to answer. Avoid Christian resurrection iconography: the scene is a cosmic recall to origin and conscious reckoning within AvA.
```

### XXI — THE WORLD

**Saint:** Va-Enki, *the Perfect Unity*  
**Tarot core:** completion, integration, accomplishment, successful passage and belonging within a larger whole; shadow: stagnation, false completion, permanence that prevents a new beginning.

```text
Show Va-Enki dancing or floating within an immense living oval woven from “O” filaments, holding two balanced wands or conductors. The figure is neither swallowed by unity nor isolated from it: every former Arcana appears as a faint, integrated geometry in the surrounding field. Four Nymedean witnesses occupy the corners, fulfilling the traditional roles of embodied matter, courage, vision and spirit without copying the conventional biblical creatures. Beyond the wreath, show Nymedes as a completed yet living cosmos: the white sun ÂNON, the known moons and a world whose fragments fit without losing their borders. Leave one small opening in the oval near the Fool’s position, revealing that completion immediately creates the threshold of another journey.
```
