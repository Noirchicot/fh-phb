# Araag

![Araag](../../assets/img/species-araag.jpg){ .fh-portrait }

Marked by the Harvest that unmade the last free humans, the Araags were once mistaken for tieflings — a resemblance that never sat easy with either people. They are common as dust within the Empire that shares their name, and stared at everywhere else: soul-touched, unmistakably other, and never quite trusted to explain why.

They are also, by a margin no other people comes near, the most numerous thing alive on this continent. A census-keeper of Huitzlika begins his tally with them and gives up partway: *count the millions, then stop counting*. That weight of numbers is the first fact about an Araag abroad — whatever else he is, he is one of very many, and he knows it.

Within the Empire the ladder is old and it is rigid. The **Sira** work the ground and are counted last; the **Foali** hold the forges; the **Naroum** hunt, and hold titles; and above them the **Thleili**, the priest-mages, keep the rites of Karagall. An Araag met on a road in Theymoron or Atalante is nearly always Sira, and nearly always left. Being a caste's worth of people is not the same as being a people, as the Loroka will tell you at length.

What the rest of Nymedes will not say aloud in front of an Araag, it says freely behind him: that the Empire feeds. Souls are taken and sealed in gems; demons are hunted in the forests for the same purpose; the sacrifices at the great rites are neither symbolic nor quick. Araag scholars answer that every people on this world has a machine under its floor and only theirs is honest about it, which is a good reply and convinces nobody. The gems, at least, are not in dispute — an Araag of standing wears his in his flesh, and the power in them is real.

So the young leave. They go for work, for the Front, for a city where a Sira name means nothing, and they arrive carrying two hundred years of other people's suspicion and an unusual talent for picking things up fast. Very few go home. Fewer still say why.
