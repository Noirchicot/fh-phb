    Silent Blade¶

    A Fate's Hand Rogue subclass — the one who ends it before it is a fight. Other rogues look for the opening in a fight; a Silent Blade arrives when there is no fight yet, and leaves before there is one. Their whole craft is the first round: get behind someone, get a hand on them, and make sure there is no second round to plan for.

    Prerequisite

    Adept in Stealth and Adept in Hunting, held on the day you take the subclass. Stealth is how you arrive; Hunting is how you knew where to wait.

    Where the points came from does not matter — free pool, species, inheritance, a level-up, a feat. Only the tiers are checked, and they are checked when you choose the subclass. Stealth is on the rogue's class list and Hunting is not, so the ordinary route buys Hunting from your free pool of 14.

    Level 3¶

    The Garrot¶

    You gain the Garrot training for free. Everyone else buys it for 1 free point and waits for level 4; you have it at 3, because a class feature does not answer to the level-4 gate. What the garrot does — no damage, only exhaustion, and a hold that is harder to break — is in Battlefield Rules.

    Dexterous Hold¶

    You grapple with Dexterity instead of Strength, and the DC to escape your grapple is 8 + your Dexterity modifier + your proficiency bonus. Your hands are quick before they are strong, and nothing about holding a person still says otherwise.

    First Blood¶

    During the first round of combat, against a creature that is surprised and that you act before in the initiative order, your first hit is a critical hit.

    One hit, not the round. The base rule already gave you Advantage there (Surprise); this makes the first blow that lands count for double — and, from level 9, for triple.

    Precision Cut¶

    While you grapple a creature with one hand and hold a dagger in the other, you may attack that creature with the dagger as a Bonus Action, and that attack has Advantage. The Advantage lasts as long as the grapple does — let go, and it is an ordinary attack again.

    Level 9¶

    Deep Cut¶

    When a Sneak Attack of yours is a critical hit, roll its damage dice three times instead of twice.

    Shadow of Death¶

    When you kill a creature and get out of sight in the same round, you gain an edge on the check to hide.

    Level 13¶

    Wider Opening¶

    Your critical range on Sneak Attacks improves by 1 again. With Sneak Critical at rogue level 2 you were already critting on a 19; from here a Sneak Attack crits on 18–20.

    Tie and Cut¶

    You may use the garrot while performing Precision Cut — the hand that holds the wire counts as the hand that holds them. Strangle and cut in the same round.

    Level 17¶

    One Shot¶

    When you land a critical hit on a creature that is below its hit point maximum and currently has fewer than 100 hit points, the creature dies.

    No damage is rolled for it, and no saving throw is offered. The two conditions are the whole rule: somebody else had to open the wound, and the thing has to be small enough to end. A creature at full health is not a target — it is a fight — and a creature still holding a hundred points is not one either.
